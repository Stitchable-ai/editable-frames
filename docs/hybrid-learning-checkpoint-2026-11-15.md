# Illustrated stories and real-mesh reflections: 15 November 2026

Research evidence, not a runtime release or production approval. The shared private learning notebook holds the films, comparisons and retained failures. These conclusions are scoped to two delivered studies on one Mac.

## Illustrated 2D stories now have a concrete example

A 34-second original office/shop/city cartoon was drawn entirely with Canvas 2D code through `defineRenderer` and `mountRenderer`. The recurring cast, illustrated sets, exact signage and charts demonstrate another code-authored video family beyond the earlier 3D fantasy examples.

Labels, captions, palette, framing and selected acting choices remain editable in preview. Changed dialogue requires rebuilding the recorded voices; cut-time edits require soundtrack alignment; a new MP4 always requires export. The full film used a custom harness and FFmpeg audio muxing. The real CLI was checked on four edited frames and a 48-frame silent specimen, not the whole film with sound.

The worker reports deterministic seeks and five edit/restores. The coordinator verified deliverable hashes, full decode and duration, and inspected the contact sheet. Neither watched normal-speed playback or listened to the soundtrack. Acting is pose-based, mouth motion follows volume, the night palette misses signs and the facepalm obscures an eye. Reuse across a second story remains untested. A watchable example does not close the broader story-production track.

Next: viewing feedback first; then a second script using the same cast if the visual direction is useful.

## Real meshes narrow the fast-reflection route

The next experiment used a third-party laptop mesh and a procedural cup. Simple per-part reflection proxies improved the laptop deck reflecting its own lid and screen. The same rule represented a ring handle as a solid box, incorrectly darkening the cup wall beside it. One proxy per entire product also lost the laptop's self-reflection.

Keep proxy rendering as a bounded preview/export candidate for slab-like parts. Use offline rendering of the same code scene for complex hollow shapes, highlights reflected between glossy surfaces and diffuse bounce light. This is a renderer choice; it does not require diffusion.

The measured comparison uses diagnostic materials, not the asset's final coatings. Hero-material previews are stills only and uncalibrated. Display reflections, edge quality, other devices and normal-speed review remain open. LIGHT1 stays partial and no default shader or integration patch is adopted.

The worker retained failed tuning passes and rendered a full 84-frame offline comparison. The coordinator decoded the comparison clips and inspected the handle failure crop; the numerical transport results were not independently recomputed in this handoff.

## Routing remains per shot

- Code scene: exact graphics, illustrated characters, controllable objects and authored motion.
- Slower rendering of that scene: complex light transport where the geometry and materials are available.
- Joint generated performance: photoreal people and products interacting when suitable rigs, captured motion or assets are unavailable.

The last route is supported by earlier interaction trials, not newly tested by these lighting experiments. These are current practical choices, not a proof that a video category is impossible with code.
